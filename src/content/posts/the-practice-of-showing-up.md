A finished piece of work rarely starts as a finished idea. An article might begin as an unclear note, a video as an observation, or a program as a hypothesis.

The practice is to stay with those beginnings long enough to learn what they could become.

## Make a small promise

Choose a practice you can return to each week: ten minutes of notes, one observation, or a small prototype after dinner.

Make the promise easy to remember and realistic enough to keep during a busy week.

## Collect before you judge

Keep the questions, problems, and phrases that catch your attention. They do not all need to become finished work.

A collection gives you something to revisit when you are ready to make something new.

## Share a little of the process

An honest attempt can help someone understand a choice, a result, or what they might try next.

You do not always need a grand conclusion. Sometimes explaining the next step is enough.
