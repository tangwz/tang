Some kinds of busyness look like progress: a full calendar, dozens of browser tabs, and a list of checked-off tasks. Yet the work you really wanted to make is still where you left it.

Try a smaller question: what deserves your full attention today?

## Start with one meaningful thing

Choose a task with a clear finish. Instead of “make progress on the project,” try “finish the opening paragraph,” “record the first shot,” or “make this endpoint work.”

A concrete goal makes it easier to begin, and easier to know when you have done enough.

## Give focus a place

You do not need an elaborate system. A quiet corner, a blank document, and a little uninterrupted time can be enough.

A boundary around that time helps you keep returning to the work when distractions arrive.

## Leave an easy place to begin again

Before you stop, write down the next action. Tomorrow, you can pick up the work without rebuilding yesterday’s context.

Those small returns turn meaningful work into a practice you can keep.
