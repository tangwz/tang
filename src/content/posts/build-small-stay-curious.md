A small project is a conversation with reality. Before the first version, you can make endless decisions about what it might become. After it exists, you can ask whether it actually helps someone.

## Make the first version useful

Small does not mean careless. Choose a narrow problem and handle it well, including the cases where something goes wrong.

For a blog, that might mean a comfortable reading experience and a clear way to find the next article. For a tool, it might mean an understandable input, a useful output, and an honest error message.

## Let constraints do some of the work

A smaller scope reduces the number of decisions you have to make. A familiar tool, a fixed afternoon, or a single page can help you finish.

When a new feature appears, return to the purpose of the project before adding it.

## Learn from real feedback

Share a version you can stand behind. Watch where people get confused and whether it helps them do what they intended.

Improve what matters. Stay curious about what happens instead of trying to make the first version perfect.
